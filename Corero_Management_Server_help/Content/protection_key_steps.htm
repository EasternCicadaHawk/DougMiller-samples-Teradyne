Essential Server Protection Practices

Your servers may encounter unwanted and malicious network traffic that includes:

  • Undesired services – Traffic from protocols or ports that you know ahead of time have no legitimate reason to access your servers.
  • DDoS attacks and competitive abuse – Attempts to make your servers unavailable to desired or legitimate traffic by overwhelming them with spurious requests. These can take a variety of forms.
  • Protocol violations and advanced evasion techniques (AETs) – These may include traffic types that are expected or otherwise legitimate, but that include packets that do not comply with the standard defined by the appropriate RFC. Such packets often indicate an attack, as they have been malformed intentionally to circumvent and exploit the protocol's correct behavior.
  • Targeted server attacks – Intrusions that are crafted specifically to exploit some characteristics of your servers in order to gain access and cause harm to your infrastructure.

Protecting your servers from such threats relies on these essential practices:

  • Allow only expected traffic – Block known malicious IP addresses and geographic locations; allow desired ports and services. The CMS supports this by enabling you to restrict access to specific clients and groups of clients. This is supported by features such as the real time reputation engine, geographic IP address blocking, on-demand shunning, IP address blocking, and IP header blocking.
  • Evaluate the amount of traffic – Limit excessive requests and connections. The CMS supports this by enabling you to configure rate limits, such as packet rate limiting.
  • Enforce the correctness of traffic – Stop protocol abuse and RFC violations, and prevent AETs. The CMS supports this with its stateful protocol validation capability.
  • Analyze the integrity of traffic – Inspect traffic and block intrusion, buffer overflows, code injections, and exploits. The CMS supports this by preventing intrusions; it identifies known attack signatures, vulnerability signatures, overflows, injections .and brute force attacks.

In addition, the CMS provides rich visibility into threats as they are recognized, assessed, and mitigated, and furnishes a variety of tools for displaying the relevant data.
